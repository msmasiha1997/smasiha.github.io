Same Training Loss,
Different Behavior:
Which Solution Should We Choose?

From choosing training solutions to learning data mixtures for LLM fine-tuning.

Saeed Masiha · 16 October 2026 · 3 min read

Which data should an LLM learn from? Answering that question means evaluating the models training produces. But a training objective can leave more than one solution to choose from.

01 / The challenge

Equal training loss can hide different outcomes.

Minimizing training loss may leave many solutions, with different validation performance. Training alone does not tell us which solution should guide the next update. In this toy example, gradient descent from different initializations approaches different points on the same set of training solutions.

The challenge is to make that choice part of the optimization.

02 / Our template

Select, then differentiate.

We propose a select-then-differentiate template: first choose a training solution using the objective we ultimately care about, then compute the hypergradient at that selected solution to guide the outer update.

For LLM data reweighting, our practical method explores candidate LoRA updates, selects the one with the lowest validation loss, then differentiates through that update to adjust the training-data mixture.

The template, in formulas

x denotes model parameters; θ controls source proportions p(θ)=softmax(θ).

1. Define training and validation losses

Training

g(θ,x)=∑sps(θ)Ls(x)

Validation

f(x)=Lval(x)

Ls is the average loss on source s; Lval is the held-out validation loss.

2. Select using validation loss

𝒮(θ)=arg minxg(θ,x) x⋆(θ)∈arg minx∈𝒮(θ)f(x)

Among training minimizers, choose the model with the lowest validation loss.

3. Differentiate at the selected solution

F(θ)=f(x⋆(θ)) ∇θF=−∇θx2g[∇xx2g]†∇xf

Evaluate every derivative at (θ,x⋆(θ)). The formula translates the validation gradient through the training curvature into a gradient for the mixture controls. The dagger (†) denotes the pseudoinverse, which handles flat training directions.

Here validation loss has no direct dependence on θ. The exact formula holds under the paper’s local PŁ and regularity assumptions, including a unique validation-optimal selection for nearby θ. The LLM animation uses finite candidate updates as a practical approximation.

Selection happens while learning the data mixture. The resulting mixture is then used for final fine-tuning, before the model is evaluated on test questions.

03 / The evidence

What changed in practice?

Higher math accuracy across four tested models. HG-MS, our select-then-differentiate method, achieved the highest reported GSM8K and MATH accuracy among completed runs on all four models. Below is the GSM8K comparison; the full results include both benchmarks.

A closer look: Qwen-2-7B

HG-MS reached 82.03% GSM8K accuracy, compared with 75.74% for ScaleBiO, the strongest comparator here. Its learned mixture also emphasized a different source: DART-Math rather than MathInstruct.

The mathematical idea

Under the paper’s local regularity assumptions, including its Polyak–Łojasiewicz condition, a unique best choice on the training-solution manifold makes the outer objective locally differentiable—even when training has many minimizers. A pseudoinverse gives the corresponding hypergradient. See the precise assumptions and result.
